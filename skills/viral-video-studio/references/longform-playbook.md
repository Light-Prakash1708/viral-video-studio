## The 5 long-form formats that work (pick one per video)
1. **Second-person science scenario** (Restart Humanity; @IfHumans premise titles): "You wake up on a Monday and something is wrong." The viewer IS the protagonist; one recurring home set (a café/street) + 2 recurring characters; ends back on the home set changed.
2. **First-person animated memoir** (Desi Divide): narrator-avatar at a recurring "home base" shot (desk + mic), cut away to illustrate every anecdote with caricature, chat-screen recreations, maps, meme inserts; ends with a personal reflection + CTA.
3. **Lore / history retelling of one overlooked character** (Kino: "The Untold Story of Ish"): chronological, calm narrator, chibi characters in a consistent world, keyword labels, resource icons ("4 ammo → food"), "2x" time-skip badge; series titles "The Complete X Story (Part 1/2/3)" are the biggest earners (1.4M).
4. **Sketch-comedy hypothetical** (@IfHumans "If Humans Paid for Air…"): premise → fake news broadcast → absurd day-in-the-life vignettes → villain CEO escalation → heist/twist → button gag. Compilations (40 min) for watch time.
5. **Myth / philosophy controversy or wonder** (@faithvsfact-en "The Original Ramayana Is TOO Brutal to Adapt" 1.1M, "X vs Y: Who Actually Won?"; @udbhava108 "What Never Dies?", "Poems That Shouldn't Be Possible"): a provocative or poetic question title, one primary text, evidence → twist → meaning.

6. **Doodle self-improvement** (@easyactually, 4.5M "losing weight is easy, actually", 2.4M "quitting your youtube addiction…", 1.8M "coding…"): all-lowercase title "<goal> is easy, actually"; black screen, yellow/blue hand-drawn stick figures, handwritten labels, pasted screenshots/photos/memes; a joke in nearly every sentence (exaggeration, "that's not even a joke", fake-reluctant asides like "if your finger slips you might accidentally end up on this website"); structure = mock the bad advice → reframe the goal ("what we actually want") → numbered principles ("10 things to know… 1… 2… so I guess we could just move on") → concrete demo (redo the conversation the right way) → simple summary card.
7. **"Every X, Explained" listicle** (@EverythingProfessor 842k "Everyday Things You DONT Understand, Explained", 718k "Every Type of Sleep…"): 13–24 min, 10–20 items. Per item (45–90 s): one-word title card → counter-intuitive claim → plain mechanism → relatable joke → "Here's the part that messes with people" twist → practical takeaway. Titles use "Every…", "…Explained", and one CAPS word (YOU, ACTUALLY, DONT).

## Storytelling engine (applies to every long-form script)
- **Cold open in 0–20 s**: a concrete scene, not a topic. Then the stakes line ("…rests on two strangers who met about 40 seconds ago").
- **Naive solution → "Except…"**: do the obvious math/plan first ("Easy."), then break it ("Except, there's a problem with generation 2."). Repeat this *problem → mechanism → example* loop 3–4 times.
- **One plain-English mechanism per section + one analogy** ("genes come in pairs… like a spare tire").
- **One historical / real case per section** with a name and a date (Habsburg Charles II, 1700; black robin "Old Blue", 1980; Pitcairn, 1790). Always add the honest caveat ("Not every scientist accepts that estimate").
- **Time-skip montages in threes**: "Year 1. A crib. Year 5. A bunk bed. Year 12. A house so loud you start sleeping in the car." with a corner YEAR counter.
- **Deadpan comedy beats every 45–60 s**: "Reportedly. Nobody has ever been able to check." "Oh no." "We should have made more friends."
- **Emotional low point at ~60%** (sunset porch: "a future or just a very slow ending"), then **"But here's the thing."** turn at ~65% into hopeful evidence.
- **Answer the title honestly at ~85%** ("Technically, maybe.") + the price + one modern data point (98-person starship crew).
- **Callback ending** on the home set (empty street → full café) + a personal question to the viewer.
- Open a loop at least once a minute ("…but that's not even the biggest problem"); close every loop before the end.

## Editing grammar
- **Science mode (calm)**: fewer hard cuts; keep a scene alive with in-scene animation, counters, labels and slow camera pushes; colour-code sections (warm home, blue diagram for mechanism, red for danger, golden sunset for emotion, green nature for hope).
- **Memoir/comedy mode (fast)**: cut on every punchline (~3–4 s); return to the home-base host shot between anecdotes as a rhythm anchor.
- On-screen text hierarchy: corner counter (YEAR 12, +75 YEARS, POPULATION 8,546,775,469), bottom stat pill ("≈10 kids per woman"), tiny bottom-left citation ("Fernandez et al. 2023"), chalkboard/diagram text only inside diagrams.
- Chapters in the description every 1–2 min; first chapter title = the hook question.

## Voice & audio
- Calm science narrator: 135–150 wpm, sentenceGap 0.25, paragraphGap 0.6 between sections, `[pause 0.5]` before punchlines and after questions; Kokoro `am_michael` speed 1.0 or `af_heart` 1.0.
- Memoir/comedy narrator: 180–200 wpm, a recurring catchphrase/self-intro, code-switching if the audience does.
- Continuous music bed (−18 relative to voice for long-form), mood changes per chapter, LRA ≤ 5 LU, master −14 LUFS; SFX on counters, swipes and punchlines only.

## Comedy toolkit (from easy, actually / Restart Humanity / If Humans)
- Deflating understatement after a big claim ("Easy." / "Oh no." / "We should have made more friends.").
- Exaggerated literalism ("you only learned how to tie a tie by watching how to tie a tie while tying a tie").
- Fake-innocent asides ("if your finger slips…", "Reportedly. Nobody has ever been able to check.").
- Redo-the-scene demos: show the wrong way, then "let's redo that" the right way with the same characters.
- Character voices / news-anchor parody for hypotheticals (@IfHumans); villain CEO as running gag.
