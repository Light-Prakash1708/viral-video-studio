You open your wallet, and every note has a timer.

Your 500 rupee note says 30 days. Your bank app says the same thing. Every rupee on Earth now expires after 30 days. When the timer hits zero the money burns. Gone.

Day 1. Total chaos. Everyone runs to spend. Malls are packed. Restaurants have three-hour waiting lists. Your neighbour buys a jet ski. He lives in Delhi.

And here's the weird part. For a while the economy looks amazing.

Economists have a name for this. The velocity of money. It's how fast money moves from hand to hand. When people hold on to cash, the economy slows down. When money has to move it races.

Shops sell out. Factories hire more workers. Everybody has a job, because everybody has to spend.

 Day 10. Prices start climbing.

Too much money is chasing too few things. A plate of momos costs double. Then triple.

And the rich realise something fast. If money can't be stored you store things instead.

Gold prices explode. Land, houses, paintings, even rare sneakers anything that won't burn becomes the new bank.

Day 20. You realise the scariest part.

You can't save anymore. Not for college. Not for your parents' hospital bills. Not for a rainy day. A savings account is now just a countdown.

So people get creative. A baker pays his rent in bread. A teacher gets paid in electricity. Neighbours start keeping notebooks of favours. "I fixed your bike, you owe me two dinners."

Without anyone planning it, people stop saving money and start saving each other.

And this isn't pure fantasy. It happened.

In 1932, during the Great Depression, a small Austrian town called Wörgl printed its own money that lost 1% of its value every month. If you kept it, it shrank. So people spent it.

Roads got built. Bridges got fixed. Unemployment fell, while the rest of the country was struggling. Other towns wanted to copy it.

 Then the national bank shut it down. Only a country is allowed to print money.

Today, in Germany, a regional currency called the Chiemgauer still uses the same idea.

But scale it to the whole planet, and things break. Banks can't lend, because loans expire too. Long-term dreams houses, businesses, retirement become almost impossible. When money can't wait, neither can people.

 Day 30. Midnight.

Across the world, a trillion notes glow orange and turn to ash.

And in the morning, a new month begins. Everyone starts again at zero. Rich and poor for one strange moment almost equal.

Now here's the twist.

Your money already expires. It's just slower.

It's called inflation. In India, prices have risen around five to six percent a year for a long time. At that rate, ₹100 today buys only about half as much in twelve years.

The timer is already running. You just can't see it.

That's why people invest. Not to get rich overnight but to stop their money from quietly burning.
