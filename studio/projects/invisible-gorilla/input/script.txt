A gorilla walked right through the middle, stopped, and beat its chest. This is the invisible gorilla experiment. In 1999, two psychologists at Harvard, Daniel Simons and Christopher Chabris, showed people a video just like this. About half of them never saw the gorilla. Not because they weren't looking. Because they were looking too hard. Your brain can't handle everything your eyes see, so it quietly throws away whatever doesn't matter for the task. Scientists call it inattentional blindness. And it's not just a party trick. It's one reason drivers miss a motorcycle, and why you can't find the keys sitting right in front of you. So what else are you missing right now?
