Why did one summer as a child feel like an entire lifetime, but an entire year now just disappears? When you were eight, one summer had everything. The first time you swam in the sea. The first scraped knee. The first firefly in a jar. Your brain saved all of it, because all of it was new. Now look at an adult year. Wake up. Commute. Work. Scroll. Sleep. Repeat. Your brain doesn't need to save the same Tuesday twice, so it doesn't. And here's the strange part. When you look back, your mind measures time by counting memories. A summer full of firsts leaves hundreds of them, so it feels huge. A year of routine leaves only a few, so it feels like it vanished. Psychologist Claudia Hammond calls this the holiday paradox. A great holiday flies by while you're living it, but in memory, it stretches out. A boring week crawls while you're in it, then shrinks to almost nothing. So time doesn't really speed up as you get older. Your days just start to look the same. Want a longer life? Collect more firsts. When was the last time you did something for the first time?
