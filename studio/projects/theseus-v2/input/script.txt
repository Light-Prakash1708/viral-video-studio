If you replace every single part of a ship, is it still the same ship? This question has been breaking brains for almost two thousand years. Back in ancient Athens, people kept the ship of the hero Theseus as a monument. But wood rots. So every time a plank went bad, they ripped it out and slapped in a brand new one. One plank at a time. Years go by, and eventually, not one original plank is left. So is it the same ship? The writer Plutarch says philosophers fought over exactly this. One side said, obviously, it's the same ship. The other side said, nope, that's a totally new ship. Then Thomas Hobbes shows up and makes it way worse. What if someone kept every old plank, and built a second ship out of them? Now you've got two ships. So which one is the real ship of Theseus? And here's the creepy part. This was never really about ships. It's about you. Your body is constantly replacing its cells. Your opinions change. Your memories change. So are you the same person you were ten years ago? Same ship, or new ship? Tell me in the comments.
