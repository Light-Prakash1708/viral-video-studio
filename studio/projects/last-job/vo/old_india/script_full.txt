In the year 2061, there was exactly one human being on Earth who still had a job.

His name was Hari Prasad. He was seventy-eight years old. And he sold tea.

Everything else had been handed to the machines. Robots drove the trains. Robots taught the children. Robots did the surgery, wrote the songs, and cooked every meal. Eight billion people, and not one of them had to work. The government paid everyone. Nobody was hungry. And nobody, it seemed, was needed.

Except for one small stall, at the end of Platform Four, in a railway station the trains no longer stopped at.

The sign said "Hari Chai. Since 1999."

Across the street, a machine made perfect tea in three seconds. For free. And still, every morning at six, twelve people stood in line for Hari.

The Ministry of Efficiency noticed. So they sent Unit M-7 to close the last human job on Earth.

M7: Your tea is thirty-one percent too sweet. Four degrees too hot. And you charge money. Why do they come?

HARI: Stay one week. Then you tell me.

So M-7 stayed. And it watched.

On Monday, Mr. Bose came. He had lost his wife the winter before. Hari didn't ask how he was. He just put two biscuits on the plate. Her favourite. Without a word.

On Tuesday, a young man named Arjun came. He hadn't left his room in months. Hari handed him a cloth. "Cups won't wash themselves, beta." Arjun stayed until noon. He smiled for the first time in a year.

On Wednesday, it was Meera. Ten years old. Maths exam.

MEERA: Extra sugar, Hari uncle?

HARI: On exam days? Always.

Every night, M-7 wrote its report. Monday said, "Inefficiency detected." Tuesday said, "Inefficiency detected." By Friday, it said something new. "Pattern unknown."

On Sunday, the seventh day, the report was due.

But the stall was closed.

Hari was in the hospital. His heart was tired. It had been working for seventy-eight years, after all.

M-7 stood by the bed. It had a question.

M7: The report is due. What do I write?

Hari smiled.

HARI: Write that you didn't find out. And then, open my stall tomorrow. Six o'clock. Twelve cups.

M7: I am not allowed to do a human job.

HARI: Then don't do it like a machine.

That night, the oldest working heart on Earth finally took a rest.

At six the next morning, the stall at Platform Four opened.

There was a robot behind the counter. And the tea was thirty-one percent too sweet. Four degrees too hot.

Mr. Bose got two biscuits. Arjun got a cloth. And Meera's cup had extra sugar, and a note in shaky robot handwriting. "Exam day. Hari says, you've got this."

That evening, M-7 sent its report to the Ministry. It was one line long.

M7: Last human job on Earth. Status... not closed.

And on the stall, it hung a new sign.

"Help wanted. Humans only."

Twelve years later, a young woman runs the busiest tea stall in the city. Her name is Meera. She remembers every customer's name. And on exam days, the sugar is always extra.

Because machines can do almost anything. But somebody still has to remember your name.
