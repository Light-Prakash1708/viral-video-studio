If every part of a ship is replaced, is it still the same ship? 
Ancient Athens kept the ship of the hero Theseus. When an old plank rotted, they swapped in fresh timber. One plank at a time. 
Years went by. Picture a day with not one original plank left. The writer Plutarch says philosophers argued about it. Some said it was the same ship. Others said it was a new one. 
Later, Thomas Hobbes made it harder. What if someone kept every old plank, and built a second ship from them? Now there are two ships. Which one is the real ship of Theseus? 
Now think about you. Your body replaces many of its cells over the years. Your opinions and your memories change too. 
So are you the same person you were ten years ago? Same ship, or new ship? Tell me in the comments.
